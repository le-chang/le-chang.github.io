**Le Chang, PhD** — computational geneticist working on **statistical genomics for precision health**. I use human genetic variation as a natural experiment to separate cause from consequence among the organ-level and molecular factors that shape aging and age-related disease, and to translate those causal signals into biomarkers, drug targets and pharmacogenomic insight. My current work is on the kidney–brain axis in neurodegeneration, and I build the open-source platforms and pipelines that make these analyses usable by others.

#### Short bio

I trained as a bioinformatics tool-builder in Jeff Xia's lab at McGill, where I was lead developer and first author of [miRNet 2.0](https://www.mirnet.ca) and [mGWAS-Explorer](https://www.mgwas.ca) and a contributing developer of [MetaboAnalyst 5.0](https://www.metaboanalyst.ca) — platforms used by hundreds of thousands of researchers. After a CPRIT postdoctoral fellowship in integrative genomics at UTHealth Houston (Zhongming Zhao), I joined Sarah Gagliano Taliun's lab at the Montreal Heart Institute / Université de Montréal as a CIHR postdoctoral fellow. There I lead cross-trait and causal genetic analyses of kidney, liver and metabolic traits with neurodegenerative disease, multi-ancestry and sex-stratified GWAS in UK Biobank, the Million Veteran Program and the Michigan Genomics Initiative, and multi-omics Mendelian randomization in the Canadian Longitudinal Study on Aging. My first-author study of Parkinson's disease and kidney-related traits appeared in *Nature Communications* (2026), and our review of kidney health and neurodegeneration in *Brain* (2025) was featured on the journal's podcast.

#### Research interests

- **Causal genetics of organ–brain axes in aging.** Genome-wide and local genetic correlation, conjunctional FDR and colocalization to find where kidney, liver and metabolic traits share causal variants with neurodegenerative disease — and Mendelian randomization to test which connections are causal.
- **Multi-omics for biomarkers and drug targets.** Integrating proteomic, metabolomic and transcriptomic QTLs with GWAS to prioritize predictive and prognostic biomarkers and genetically validated therapeutic targets, including drug-target and drug-response genes, as a route to pharmacogenomics and personalized medicine.
- **Multi-ancestry and sex-stratified genomics.** Large-scale GWAS meta-analysis of kidney and urinary traits (hematuria, eGFR, urinary electrolytes) across ancestries and sexes, so that genomic discoveries translate equitably.
- **Open bioinformatics platforms and reproducible pipelines.** Web tools and Nextflow workflows that let clinicians and researchers run these genomic analyses without writing code.
